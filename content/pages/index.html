02 — The Atelier

A Craft Measured
in Months, Not Machines

No two rugs leave our atelier the same. A single piece may occupy a loom for four to nine months, tied knot by knot by hands that learned the craft from their mothers, and theirs before them.

We work only in hand-carded wool, mulberry silk, and vegetable dye — nothing that cannot be traced to a single village, a single family, a single loom.

The Process

Five Stages, One Standard

  1. I

    Hand Knotting

    Every knot is tied by hand, knot by knot and row by row, at up to 400 knots per square inch.

  2. II

    Washing

    The reverse is carefully singed before the rug is river-washed and sun-dried to remove impurities, soften the fibres, and settle the dyes.

  3. III

    Stretching

    The washed rug is stretched and aligned by hand to restore precise dimensions and ensure an even, balanced shape.

  4. IV

    Finishing

    The pile is hand-clipped, the edges are bound, both surfaces are cleaned, loose threads are trimmed, and the rug is thoroughly vacuumed.

  5. V

    Inspection

    Each piece is inspected by three artisans before it earns our mark.

03 — Our World

Editorial Notes from the Loom

Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh

Where Wool Learns Patience

In the quiet hours before sunrise, the looms begin their slow arithmetic. A weaver ties, on average, eleven thousand knots before lunch — a rhythm passed down rather than taught.

The Ganges Riverbank

The Wash That Ends
the Beginning

Every rug is washed by hand along the riverbank, a final rite that softens the pile and reveals the depth of vegetable dye for the first time.

04 — Heritage

A Lineage in Wool

  1. 1962

    The First Loom

    Raw wool was sourced from Panipat and Bikaner, brought to Bhadohi, and supplied to local manufacturers. A single loom was then installed, marking the beginning of our own production house.

  2. 1988

    A Second Generation

    An in-house hand-dyeing unit was established and the design process was codified. More looms followed, and the atelier grew to seventeen master weavers.

  3. 2009

    First Commission Abroad

    A private residence in London becomes the first piece to leave India for a Western interior.

  4. Today

    A Third Generation Continues

    Rugs De Indiska now furnishes private collections across five continents, each piece still tied by hand.
